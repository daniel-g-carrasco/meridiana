# PVsyst in practice: forums, Q&A, regulators and validation studies, and what they mean for Meridiana's defaults and checks

Research report for **Meridiana**, the open-source desktop PV design program (Python, PySide6/Qt 6, pvlib, GPL-3.0-or-later) whose goal is at least every PVsyst function, with Aurora Solar as the UX reference.

Research date: 22 October 2026. The research was read-only: no accounts, logins, sign-ups, posts, forms, installers or API keys, and no personal data sent anywhere.

## 0. Scope, sources and how to read this report

### 0.1 What this report adds to report 06

Report `docs/studio/06-pvsyst-aurora-pvlib.md` inventories what the PVsyst 8.1 help *says*. This report collects what users and PVsyst staff *do and argue about* in practice:

- the values practitioners really enter, and the debates around the defaults;
- what PVsyst staff answer on the official forum (often more candid than the help);
- bugs and behaviour changes of the 8.0.x line, and the structural limits users keep hitting;
- what lenders, independent engineers and one regulator (Brazil's EPE) check;
- Italian practice, from public PVsyst reports filed in environmental assessments and from the agrivoltaic rules;
- validation studies against measured data and other tools;
- the exact staff wording on the interpolation of user-defined IAM profiles.

Facts already in report 06 are not repeated; cross-references point to its sections (for example "06 §1.1.13.8").

### 0.2 Method and access limits

**PVsyst forum (forum.pvsyst.com).**
- Every page of the live forum, including `robots.txt`, the RSS feeds and the sitemap, returns HTTP 405 with an AWS WAF "Human Verification" CAPTCHA, both to WebFetch and to plain `curl`. It was not bypassed.
- The forum content was read from the public **Wayback Machine** captures (web.archive.org, raw mode), as the brief allowed for archives. The Wayback CDX index lists **4,637 archived topic URLs** (topic ids 2 to 4826, captures 2022 to April 2026; most topics have a 2025 or 2026 capture).

### 0.3 Conventions

- **Staff** / **member**: the role of the forum author whose post supports the statement. "Staff" statements are authoritative for PVsyst's behaviour at their date, not necessarily for 8.1.
- **Confidence**: **H** = staff statement or official document, consistent with other sources and not contradicted by a later version; **M** = single staff statement that may be dated, or a regulator or industry benchmark, or several consistent member reports; **L** = single member or vendor statement, marked **[unverified]**.
- **(analysis)** marks the researcher's own reasoning and every proposal for Meridiana.
- Dates are the dates of the posts or documents (YYYY-MM-DD). Quotes are verbatim and at most 25 words.
- Forum URLs are the canonical topic URLs of the forum (`https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/<id>-<slug>/`). They are behind the CAPTCHA today; each one has Wayback captures.

### 0.4 Executive summary

1. **Defaults are judgement calls, and PVsyst staff say so.** The module quality default (a quarter of the tolerance span) is "our own choice"; the 1% mismatch default of 2016-2017 was "an estimation" (the 8.1 help now uses a string-length table up to 2%); LID was "around 2%, this is the default in PVsyst" in 2016 but has no default in the 8.1 help; for soiling, the founder knew "no general study nor methodology". Meridiana should show every default with its origin, as it already does with its pedigree (§1, §2).
2. **The largest silent yield inflators are in the PAN file, not in the loss settings.** Staff repeatedly warn that manufacturer and "certified lab" PAN files carry boosted IAM profiles (up to 3 points of yield in one 2025 case) and boosted low-light efficiency (corrected lab data stay between -3% and -2% at 200 W/m²). PVsyst's own database refuses Pnom/Imp·Vmp gaps above 0.2%, RshExp above 5.5, and outdoor IAM data since 6.53. Meridiana's PAN import should run the same checks automatically (§1 items 1-6 and 13-14, §5).
3. **Degradation is the most misunderstood input.** Warranty curves are a worst case per module, not the plant average; staff recommend about 0.4 %/yr (0.3 for n-type), LID only for p-type, LID = first-year warranty loss minus half the annual rate, and the "Module warranty" fields do nothing in the simulation (§1 items 16-18).
4. **Practice benchmarks exist.** Brazil's EPE published the loss and uncertainty values certified for 1,616 auction projects (2017-2022): IAM 0.9-1.6% (0.6% mean in 2022), soiling mostly 1-2%, LID mostly 1-3%, mismatch about 1.3%, degradation below 0.6 %/yr, DC ohmic about 1%, long-term combined uncertainty 5.5-7.2% for the middle half of projects. Italian PVsyst reports filed for environmental assessment use Meteonorm, albedo 0.20, mismatch 2% + 0.2%, module quality gains of 0.4-0.8% and soiling from 1% up to 5% (§2, §7).
5. **P50/P90 in PVsyst is a simple Gaussian on annual values**, and staff are explicit about what not to do: no hourly or monthly P90, no concatenation of one-year P90s, no "P90 weather file" shortcut, no year-by-year uncertainty growth. Users keep asking for 25-year Pxx tables, a resource-uncertainty field, PR at P90 and Monte Carlo. Fleet data show P50 estimates running 3% to 13% high (§6).
6. **PVsyst 8.0 changed results**, mainly in shading and bifacial: rear sky diffuse now from the real rows (one case +44% to +20.6% rear diffuse gain; staff: V7.4 was "unrealistically high"), stricter orientation checks, tracker diffuse shading needing a representative tracker, and a long series of shading, bifacial, PVGIS-import and report fixes from 8.0.5 to 8.0.20 (§3).
7. **Hourly steps hide clipping.** A Chinese study found 2.15% clipping hourly against 2.45% at 15 minutes (DC/AC 1.3); PVsyst 8.0 added a sub-hourly clipping correction and 8.1 sub-hourly simulation (§1 item 29, §3).
8. **Italian specifics.** The DM Agrivoltaico rules require the 60% producibility check with **PVGIS (SARAH 2, 14% losses)**, not PVsyst, while bankable design uses the designer's tools; SARAH 2 is the PVGIS 5.2 database, so the rule and current PVGIS 5.3 (SARAH3) no longer match. No source shows CEI 0-21/0-16 being handled inside PVsyst (§7).
9. **IAM interpolation (answer to the specific question).** Staff wrote in 2013 that PVsyst "adjusts a cubic function on the points #1, #2, #3, #4" for a value between #2 and #3, and in 2017 that after the 6.40-6.42 spline artefact "all the points interpolations up to the first <1 are computed with a linear interpolation". Meridiana's rule matches this reading. No later statement, no 8.x release note and no line of the 8.1 help describe or change it: PVsyst 8 behaviour is **[unverified]**, with no evidence of change (§9).

### 3.5 Structural limits users keep hitting

From staff answers (dates in §10.1), PVsyst cannot:

- put strings of different lengths, or modules of different power, on one MPPT, or mix orientations inside a string; more than two orientations in parallel on one MPPT;
- compute backtracking aware of tracker heights on terrain, or different backtracking for morning and afternoon;
- model cascaded MV transformers, three-winding transformers in the single-line diagram, aluminium and parallel conductors in the detailed DC wiring tool (2018);
- give partial unavailability per sub-array, an exact availability energy loss, or a flat unavailability %;
- use hourly measured albedo (V8 stores MET albedo as monthly values), measured rear-side POA, or front POA directly (it is reverse-transposed);
- run the bifacial model on polygonal fields, single tables, mixed pitches or mixed module sizes in one orientation;
- report annual shading loss per module or table, results per sub-array, or more than 36 yearly hourly files from the ageing tool;
- export all variant parameters in a parseable form;
- hide manufacturers in the database (only favourites);
- model snow, LeTID, tracker pointing errors (staff: add them to the P90 uncertainty), or flow batteries.

The 8.1 time-series inputs ease some of them (albedo CSV from 8.1.2, soiling CSV from 8.1.0, unavailability CSV from 8.1.6, hourly snow coverage through soiling; 06 §1.11.3), but the forum statements above predate 8.1 and were not re-tested. Each item is a candidate differentiator for Meridiana (analysis); §4 lists the requests behind them.

### 6.4 P50/P90 rules stated by PVsyst staff

- Annual yields are assumed Gaussian; the P50 equals the simulated E_Grid when the weather input is a TMY or a multi-year average; P90 = P50·(1 - 1.28σ), P75 = P50·(1 - 0.674σ) (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/2880-median-p50-ac-energy-vs-ac-energy-injected-into-the-grid/, 2022; https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/3252-p75-evaluation-for-25-years-simulation/, 2023).
- The "climate change" shift moves only the P50 (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/2880-median-p50-ac-energy-vs-ac-energy-injected-into-the-grid/, 2024-08-15).
- No P90 for hourly, daily or monthly values; no "P90 hourly file" by scaling (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/1624-power-generation-data-of-probability-p90p70-etc/, 2018; https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/2915-p50-and-p90-userhourly-csv/, 2025-07-03).
- Do not concatenate one-year P90s: over n years the weather variability shrinks roughly as 1/√n, the other uncertainties do not (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/3365-p50p90/, 2023-07-27); the P99/P50 ratio of year 0 applies to every year (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/4086-p99-value-for-the-nth-year/, 2024-10-11).
- Staff doubt providers' "P90 weather files" and P90 simulations (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/3252-p75-evaluation-for-25-years-simulation/, 2023-05-17); when a P90 file is used anyway, members set the variability to 0 and keep the other uncertainties (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/2816-adding-tmy-p90-files-from-meteo-databases/, 2022, member).
- PR at P90 equals the P50 PR to first order (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/4621-how-to-account-for-climate-variability-when-determining-pr-under-p90-conditions/, 2025-08-28).

Meridiana proposal (analysis): implement these rules for parity; add (a) a year-by-year Pxx table with σ_total(n) = √(σ_weather²/n + σ_other²), (b) an empirical Pxx from the PVGIS 2005-2023 hourly series next to the Gaussian one (the METU study found the Gaussian over-predicting P90 by about 4%: §1 item 41), and (c) a labelled resource-uncertainty input.

Observations **(analysis)**:
- **Meteonorm dominates the yield studies**, often with old irradiation periods (1986-2005, 1989-2003, 1991-2006). The founder noted in 2012 that European irradiation had risen by about 5% since 2000 in all datasets (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/81-what-is-the-accuracy-of-the-different-meteo-data/), so old periods tend to be conservative; Meridiana's PVGIS 5.3 (SARAH3 2005-2023) is more recent. ### 7.2 The agrivoltaic producibility rule: PVGIS, not PVsyst

The DM Agrivoltaico rules (DM MASE 22 December 2023 n. 436, operating rules, Allegato 1, §2.B.3 "Producibilità elettrica minima", pp. 21-22) were drafted by GSE and approved by MASE (a departmental decree of 16 May 2024, n. 233, according to a search-engine snippet of https://www.reteambiente.it/normativa/54295/ [secondhand]). They require:

- FV_agri ≥ 0.6 × FV_standard, both at the same site;
- the reference plant: modules with 20% efficiency on fixed supports facing south, tilt = latitude minus 10°;
- **both yields computed with PVGIS**: database "SARAH 2", system losses 14%, ground mounting; the agrivoltaic plant with its own mounting and orientation;
- correction factors on the agrivoltaic PVGIS result: +15% for bifacial modules, +15% for two-axis trackers, +30% for both;
- footnote 4: PVGIS is used to give homogeneous and verifiable figures for the competitive procedures; the project's own sizing, "anche per stabilire criteri di bancabilità", uses the designers' tools.

Source: https://www.mase.gov.it/portale/documents/d/guest/roagr-allegato-1-pdf (read 2026-10-22; Italian). Elettricità Futura's comments on the draft (21 July 2023) had suggested leaving the software to the proponent (SolarGIS or PVsyst named) and raising the electrical losses to 16%: https://www.elettricitafutura.it/Policy/Transizione-energetica-e-procedure-autorizzative/Regole-Operative-per-i-sistemi-agrivoltaici_5355.html.

Points for Meridiana **(analysis)**:
- The rule names **SARAH 2**, a PVGIS 5.2 database, while PVGIS 5.3 (the default since September 2024) uses SARAH3 (06 §3.4). Meridiana should run the check with the database the rule names when the API still offers it, otherwise say which one it used. Whether 2025 updates of the rules changed this was not checked [to verify with the current MASE text].
- The rules give the reference plant the same peak power as the agrivoltaic plant, while the Grottaglie report sized it from the fenced area (0.9 ha/MWp) and quoted 14% efficiency. FV is defined per hectare, so the interpretation matters: Meridiana should compute the ratio both ways and show the rule's text.
- The DM Agrivoltaico regulations were not in Daniel's local archive (`reference\laws`) on 22/10/2026.

## 9. The interpolation of user-defined IAM profiles

### 9.1 What PVsyst staff wrote (exact wording)

The live forum is behind a CAPTCHA; both threads were read from Wayback Machine captures of their new URLs.

**1. "IAM Profile - only 9 points?"**, Problems/Bugs, https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/334-iam-profile-only-9-points/ (old URL https://forum.pvsyst.com/viewtopic.php?t=1267), reply by **André Mermoud (Moderators, PVsyst founder)** on **2013-10-14**:

- "PVsyst performs a cubic interpolation between points."
- "for a value situated between point #2 and #3, it adjusts a cubic function on the points #1, #2, #3, #4."
- He adds, in substance: 9 points are largely sufficient for a smooth IAM curve; the "external" point can create artefacts; to avoid them, move point #8 to a higher angle (lower IAM), or drop point #4 or #5 ("quasi useless") and add a point between #8 and #9.

**2. "IAM change in PVsyst versions - unexpected result"**, Problems/Bugs, https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/1195-iam-change-in-pvsyst-versions-unexpected-result/ (old URL https://forum.pvsyst.com/viewtopic.php?t=2698), reply by **André Mermoud (Moderators)** on **2017-02-09**, to a user who saw different IAM results between 6.42 and 6.52:

- "From version 6.40 to 6.42, the IAM profile was indeed computed with a cubic spline between point#2 and point#3"
- "which may lead to an artefact curve depending on how the points are aligned (if Point#1 and Point#2 are 1)."
- "This could give a discrepancy up to 0.3% to 0.4% in some cases."
- "Now all the points interpolations up to the first <1 are computed with a linear interpolation."

A member asked on 2017-02-20 what "up to the first <1" means exactly; the latest capture (12 September 2025) shows no staff answer. Another member summarised that the issue concerns only customised profiles (PAN or detailed losses), not ASHRAE.

**Related official text.** The 6.43 release notes list among fixed errors a "Slight difference in IAM profile interpretation when reading internal DB or PAN file. May lead to slightly different IAM losses" (https://www.pvsyst.com/help/release-notes/index.html), which dates the change to 6.43 (published 17 March 2016; release dates from the same page).

### 9.4 Meridiana's implementation and what to keep (analysis)

`src/meridiana/energy/simulate.py::profile_iam` joins the points with a straight line up to the first point below 1, then with a cubic through the four points around the angle (Lagrange form, points j to j+3 with j = k-1 clipped to the valid range), clips to [0, 1], appends (90°, 0) when missing and normalises to the value at 0°. This matches the staff descriptions:

- **Linear part.** "Up to the first <1" read as: every segment whose upper end is at or before the first point below 1 is linear, which includes the segment from the last point equal to 1 to the first point below 1. Meridiana does exactly this (`k >= below[0]` switches to the cubic). This is the natural reading, but the opposite reading (cubic already on that segment) cannot be excluded [unverified].
- **Cubic part.** For a value between points k and k+1, the 2013 text fits a cubic through k-1, k, k+1, k+2, which is Meridiana's choice; at the first and last segments PVsyst's choice of points is not documented. Meridiana shifts the window inside the table (points 1-4 or n-3 to n), a reasonable choice [unverified].
- **Points above 1.** PVsyst refuses profiles above 1 (users could not represent an optimum away from normal incidence: https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/2226-iam-user-profile-with-optimisation-for-angles-not-0%C2%B0/, 2020); Meridiana normalises to IAM(0°) and clips at 1, which is consistent.

Proposals:
1. Keep the rule; label it in the pedigree as "PVsyst 6.43+ rule as described by its staff (2013, 2017); not documented for 8.x".
2. Add unit tests for: a profile with two leading 1s (the 6.40-6.42 artefact case), a profile starting below 1 at 30°, the last segment (85-90°), and a flat profile of 1 (must give zero loss on beam and diffuse).
3. Run the Fresnel bounds check of §5 rule 1 before using any profile, as PVsyst and PVEL recommend; this matters more than the interpolation (2013-2017 interpolation effects were 0.3-0.4%, boosted profiles 1-3 points).
4. Read the "active" flag of the user profile from the PAN file: PVsyst stores whether the user profile is enabled, and a disabled profile falls back to the default (https://forum.pvsyst.com/topic/4274-issue-in-user-defined-iam-values-importing/, 2025-01-29, staff).
